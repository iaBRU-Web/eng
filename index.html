The Problem with Re-Reading

Unit 6 of your English S4 textbook introduces you to effective study skills. One of the most important insights is that re-reading — though it feels productive — is actually one of the least effective ways to learn.

❌ Re-Reading

The passive method

  • Your eyes scan the page again
  • Words feel familiar, so it feels like learning
  • Your brain stays passive — no new work done
  • Information fades quickly after the test
  • Wastes time — you cover the same ground
  • Creates an illusion of knowledge
✅ Note-Taking & Summarizing

The active method

  • You process and rewrite in your own words
  • Your brain makes new connections
  • Forces you to identify what is important
  • Strengthens long-term memory
  • Notes become a tool you can study from
  • Creates real understanding
2

The Science: Why Active Learning Works

Unit 6 teaches you to work with language — not just look at it. This is grounded in how the human brain stores information. When you actively process what you read, your brain encodes it more deeply.

🧠 Brain Science

The "Encoding" Principle

Your brain remembers information better when it is connected to something you did, not just something you saw. Writing a note or summary forces your brain to retrieve, process, and reconstruct meaning — which is exactly the kind of activity that builds long-term memory.

What Good Note-Taking Looks Like

According to the study skills framework in Unit 6, good note-taking involves several steps:

1

Read the whole text first

Get the overall meaning before you start taking notes. This is called reading for gist.

2

Identify the main idea of each paragraph

Every paragraph in a well-written text has a topic sentence. Find it and write it in your own words.

3

Write key words and phrases — not full sentences

Notes should be short. Use abbreviations, symbols (→, =, &), and your own shorthand. If you copy full sentences, you are not taking notes — you are re-writing.

4

Organise notes under headings

Unit 6 emphasises the importance of headings and subheadings in writing. Use the same structure in your notes to keep information grouped clearly.

5

Review notes without looking at the text

Close the book and try to explain the content using only your notes. If you can, you have understood it.

4

Summary Writing: Notes in Full Sentences

A summary is different from notes: it is a shortened version of the original text, written in full sentences and in your own words. Unit 6 links this skill to the Standard Paragraph structure taught throughout the textbook.

A good summary must:

  • Include only the most important ideas
  • Be significantly shorter than the original
  • Use your own words, not copied phrases
  • Follow the original order of ideas
  • Not include your personal opinion

Study Skill Tips from Unit 6

Unit 6 trains you in school language functions — the academic phrases and structures used when writing and studying. These are tools that make your note-taking more precise:

🔑

Use topic sentences

The first sentence of each paragraph usually contains the main idea. Find it; note it.

🏷

Label your notes

Use headings from the original text to organise your notes — just like Unit 6 teaches about subheadings.

⚡

Be concise

Remove all examples, repeated ideas, and filler words. Keep only the core meaning.

🔗

Use linking words

Unit 6 covers connectives. Use them in summaries: "therefore", "however", "as a result".

📋

Review after class

Within 24 hours, review your notes and add anything you missed while the lesson is still fresh.

🗣

Work in groups

Unit 6 specifically covers working in groups. Compare your notes with classmates to fill gaps.

Key Vocabulary — Unit 6

These are important terms from Unit 6's vocabulary section that relate to study skills and note-taking:

TermDefinitionHow it relates to note-taking
Summary A short account giving the main points of something The goal of summarizing is to distil — keep only what matters
Extract A short passage taken from a longer text Unit 6 uses extracts from literature as practice material for note-taking
Heading A title or subheading that introduces a section Use headings in your own notes to organise ideas clearly
Interpret To understand and explain the meaning of something Good notes interpret — they don't just copy; they explain in new words
Instruction A direction or command telling you how to do something Unit 6 teaches reading and using instructions — a note-taking exercise in itself
Concise Giving much information clearly in a few words Notes must be concise — long notes defeat the purpose
Paraphrase To express the meaning of something using different words Paraphrasing is the key skill in both note-taking and summarizing
